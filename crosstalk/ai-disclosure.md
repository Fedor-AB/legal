# Crosstalk — AI disclosure

> Draft for review. Not in force until Fedor AB publishes it with an effective date.

## What Crosstalk tells you

The hosts you hear on Crosstalk are not real people. An AI language model writes what they say, and an AI speech system produces their voices. Nobody records, reviews or edits a host segment before you hear it.

Because the hosts are generated, what they say about an artist, a song, the date or the weather can be wrong. Treat it as entertainment, not as a source of facts.

The music is different. Crosstalk plays your own Apple Music through your own subscription, and it does not generate or change the music.

## In-app texts

The designer decides how these texts look and where they stand, within the requirements in the next section.

**First notice** (on the screen that asks for your permission, before the first host segment plays):

> Crosstalk's hosts are created by AI.

**Label** (on the switch for the hosts in Settings):

> AI Hosts

[The three texts below are proposed and are confirmed when they are built.]

**About text** (Settings):

> Crosstalk's hosts are generated by artificial intelligence. Their scripts are written by an AI language model and their voices are made by AI speech synthesis. The hosts are fictional characters. They are not real people and do not speak for any artist, label or broadcaster. Host segments are generated automatically and are not checked by a person, so they can contain mistakes. The music you hear is your own Apple Music and is not generated or altered by Crosstalk.

**Lock screen and other system players** (title shown while a host segment plays):

> Crosstalk — AI host

**App Store description** (one line):

> The hosts are AI-generated: their scripts and voices are synthetic.

## What the disclosure must achieve

The designer decides where each text goes. Whatever the layout, these five things must hold.

1. **Before the first host segment.** A listener sees the first notice no later than the first time a host speaks. Article 50(5) of the EU AI Act requires the information "at the latest at the time of the first interaction or exposure".
2. **Clear and distinguishable.** The notice is ordinary readable text on the screen itself, not a line inside the terms of use and not hidden behind a link. The same article requires a "clear and distinguishable manner".
3. **Findable later.** The switch for the hosts in Settings carries the label, so a listener who skipped past the first notice can still tell.
4. **Accessible.** Every text is real text that VoiceOver reads and Dynamic Type scales, with sufficient contrast. Article 50(5) requires the information to "conform to the applicable accessibility requirements".
5. **Covers both script and voice.** The wording says that the hosts are made by AI, which covers their words and their voices. A label that says only "AI voice" is not enough.

## Screen-off listening

Much listening happens with the screen off, in a car or over a speaker, and other people in the room hear the hosts without ever seeing the app. [To be confirmed when built: the lock-screen title above covers that case.]

A spoken notice at the start of a listening session is not part of the first release.

## Marking inside the audio

Every host segment is also marked as AI-generated in two ways that software can read:

- **A watermark in the sound itself.** The hosts' voices are produced by Google's Gemini speech models, and Google embeds its SynthID watermark in the audio they generate.
- **A label in the audio file.** Crosstalk writes an "AI-generated" statement into the metadata of each audio file it delivers to the app. The app plays that file and does not hand it out.

**How to check a segment.** If you have a file with a Crosstalk host segment, you can check it with Google's SynthID Detector (synthid.com). The detector accepts audio files; it asks you to sign in and to accept Google's terms. It reports that the audio was made with Google's AI; it does not name Crosstalk. In November 2026 we checked host segments as Crosstalk delivers them, and re-encoded copies of them, and the detector found the watermark in each. We have not tested a screen recording or a recording made through a microphone.

## Languages

The first notice and the label are shown in the app's interface language. English is the reference text.
